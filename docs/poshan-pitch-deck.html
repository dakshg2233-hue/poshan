01PROBLEM

The measurement is wrong before the advice begins

India is being read on somebody else’s scale.

Body Mass Index was calibrated on European bodies. Asian-Indians carry more visceral fat, and carry it earlier, at a lower weight. So the ICMR and the WHO Asia-Pacific consensus move the line: overweight begins at 23.0, not 25.0. Obesity at 27.5, not 30.0.

Every mainstream tracker an Indian household can download still draws the line at 25. Between 23.0 and 25.0 sits a population that is metabolically at risk and is being told, by the app on their phone, that they are fine.

Fig. 1 — Two centimetres of scale, one national blind spot. Poshan’s entire product sits on the left-hand line.

Three people are failed at the same moment

THE PATIENT

Gets advice they cannot cook

“Eat healthy” arrives as quinoa, kale and grilled chicken breast. The household cooks dal, chawal, roti and sabzi. The plan is abandoned at the kitchen door — not from non-compliance, but from irrelevance.

THE CLINICIAN

Has nothing between the prescription and “eat less rice”

An endocrinologist writes metformin in ninety seconds and has no tool that turns an HbA1c of 8.4 into a Tuesday dinner. Dietetics referral is a paper sheet, unfollowed and unmeasured.

THE HOSPITAL

Runs dietetics as an unauditable cost centre

No record of what was advised, by whom, or whether the patient opened it. Nothing to show an accreditor, nothing to bill against, nothing to improve on.

02PRODUCT

What we built

A clinical plate, in the food the house already cooks.

Poshan reads height and weight against Asian-Indian cutoffs, then draws a thali — the actual round plate, with the actual katoris — portioned for that person’s band, goal, diet and region. In Hindi or English. It is a nutrition tool, not medical advice, and it says so on every screen.

One engine, two products, sold to two buyers who reinforce each other.

B2C — ₹299 / month · ₹2,499 / year

Poshan Home

BMI on Indian cutoffs, a portioned thali, 40 dishes with full nutrition and recipes, four biomarkers logged by hand, meal-photo scanning, condition support for diabetes, PCOS, thyroid and anaemia, up to six family profiles, and a registered dietitian reviewing the plan monthly.

B2B — ₹999 / ₹3,999 / ₹14,999 per month

Poshan for Clinics

The clinician enters lab values — HbA1c, creatinine, eGFR, haemoglobin, lipids. Poshan drafts a plan from the food that region actually cooks. The clinician approves it before the patient ever sees it, and the sign-off is logged against their registration number with a timestamp.

What is already in the box

  • Asian-Indian band engine — ten BMI bands on ICMR cutoffs, each mapped to a portioned plan.
  • 40 dishes with complete macro and micronutrient profiles, full recipes, regional and veg/non-veg variants.
  • An 11-condition safety checker with a documented conflict matrix — it catches the case where a renal-safe plan collides with an anaemia-corrective one.
  • Clinician sign-off, logged — registration number and timestamp on every plan released to a patient. This is the feature that makes Poshan sellable into a hospital.
  • Meal-photo scanning on the Claude vision API — photograph the plate, get items and calories back.
  • Hindi and English throughout, including the patient-facing plan and the printed discharge sheet.
  • Row-level security on every table — no account can read another account’s health data at the database layer.

The unique selling proposition, in three layers

Poshan is the only nutrition product where an Indian clinician’s signature sits between the algorithm and the patient, on food the household already cooks, measured against Indian cutoffs.

Each layer alone is a feature. Stacked, they are the reason a hospital signs a purchase order.

  • Calibration — 23.0, not 25.0. Everything downstream is re-based: the band, the calorie deficit, the portion, the escalation threshold. A competitor adopting this must invalidate every plan they have ever issued.
  • Cultural — katori, not cup. Portions in household measures, dishes by region, nutrition computed on Indian preparation. A plan a family can cook on a Tuesday is the only plan that gets followed.
  • Clinical — the signature is a logged event. No plan reaches a patient un-approved, and every approval carries a registration number and a timestamp. This is what makes Poshan procurable rather than merely downloadable, and it is what turns the doctor into the distribution channel.

Unfair advantages: what we hold, and what we must go and get

Say this plainly rather than being caught out

Poshan does not yet hold a single unfair advantage. The cutoffs are published national guidance. Forty dishes, two languages and the sign-off flow are all copyable by a funded competitor. What exists today is a six-to-twelve month head start and a product that is finished while others are still arguing about scope. A head start is worth funding. It is not a moat, and calling it one in a room full of investors ends the meeting.

Five genuine moats are available, in the order they should be acquired:

  • Regulatory standing. ABDM / ABHA certification plus demonstrable DPDP compliance. Months of legal and integration work that every rival must repeat, and increasingly the gate on hospital and government procurement. Cheapest moat on the list — buy it first.
  • A published outcomes study. HbA1c delta at twelve weeks against standard counselling, peer-reviewed and Indian. Cannot be copied, only replicated — which costs a rival their own patients and a year. It is simultaneously the sales asset, the insurance unlock and the multiple justification.
  • Clinician switching costs. Once patient history, plan templates and the approval ledger live in Poshan, leaving means abandoning a regulated record. Records are the stickiest thing in health software; the sign-off ledger is already built, so this compounds from launch if the data model is right.
  • Exclusive channel contracts. Insurance and pharma agreements carrying category exclusivity. One signed insurer removes a channel from every competitor at once.
  • The approve-and-edit corpus. Every plan a clinician accepts, edits or rejects is a labelled correction from a qualified Indian professional. That is a training signal no consumer app can buy and no new entrant can bootstrap — and at ten thousand clinicians it is the one advantage on this list that is genuinely unfair.

Note the ordering: the first three are bought with this round. The last two need the scale that the first three unlock. A moat is a purchase order, not a plan.

07GO TO MARKET

How the money gets spent

Indian clinical software is sold in the corridor.

No diabetologist has ever bought practice software from a landing page. They buy it from a person who showed up on a Tuesday afternoon between patients, demonstrated it on a real lab report, and came back on Thursday. This is a field motion, and it is the thing the round is for.

The motion, end to end

  • Source — inside sales work the specialist lists: 6,233 diabetologists nationally, plus endocrinology, nephrology and obstetrics, plus every hospital dietetics department in eight metros.
  • Demo — a field rep sits in the clinic and enters that clinician’s own patient’s HbA1c. The plan drafts in front of them. This is a four-minute demonstration and it is the entire sale.
  • Trial — fourteen days at the Practitioner tier, with a clinical success manager onboarding their first ten patients by hand so the account is never empty.
  • Land — ₹999 a month is below the threshold at which an Indian consultant needs anyone’s approval to sign. That price is deliberate.
  • Expand — Practitioner to Clinic when the second consultant asks for a seat; Clinic to Hospital when the dietetics department wants departments and audit. Expansion, not new logos, is where the second year of revenue comes from.

Eight metros, in this order

Delhi NCR and Mumbai first — highest specialist density and the corporate-hospital chains whose approval other cities copy. Then Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai, Ahmedabad, Kolkata. Maharashtra alone holds 1,127 diabetologists and Tamil Nadu 727, so two of the first four cities sit on the deepest lists in the country.

What the marketing agency is actually for

Not brand advertising. Three jobs, all of them in service of the sales motion:

  • Hindi-first performance — the consumer funnel, run on Meta, Google and YouTube in Hindi and English, with a hard ₹720 blended CAC ceiling and monthly cohort reporting.
  • Clinician credibility content — the case studies, the conference presence at Diabetes India, ESICON and IDACON, and the KOL programme that gets twenty respected consultants using Poshan publicly. A rep who can name three peers already on the platform closes at roughly twice the rate of one who cannot.
  • Regional-language production — the patient-facing video and print assets that a clinic hands out, in the language of the clinic’s catchment.

09GAPS

What more we need to grow and scale

The honest list of what is not built yet.

Sales and marketing are the right things to fund and they are not sufficient on their own. Three tiers of work sit behind the number, and the first tier is not optional — nothing else in this document happens until it is done.

Tier 1 · Ship blockers · 0–90 days · before a single rupee arrives
  • Go live. Authentication and persistence are not fully wired — a signed-in user can still lose profile data on refresh. This is the only thing standing between a finished product and a revenue-generating one.
  • Move payment verification server-side. The Razorpay signature check currently reads keys on the client and the edge function is written but not deployed. Do not take money until this ships. It is a genuine security defect, not a nice-to-have.
  • DPDP Act 2023 compliance. Health data is sensitive personal data under Indian law. Consent architecture, a named Data Protection Officer, breach procedure and data-processing agreements. No hospital procurement will clear without it.
  • A test suite. There is currently zero coverage, and the profile data-loss bug is exactly the class a single test catches. An enterprise buyer’s security review will ask.
  • Point the scanner at production. The vision route still defaults to a localhost endpoint.
  • Ship one palette. The palette switcher is a development tool currently shipping to real visitors. Choose Sindoor, build its dark variant, delete the other nine.
Tier 2 · Scale unlocks · 3–12 months · funded by this round
  • Lab report parsing. Today a clinician types HbA1c in by hand. Parsing a Dr Lal PathLabs or Metropolis PDF into structured values removes the single largest friction in the clinical workflow and is the difference between a tool used weekly and one used daily. Highest return of anything on this list.
  • ABDM / ABHA certification. Already declared as in-certification. It is the enterprise unlock, the credibility marker, and increasingly a procurement precondition for government-linked and accredited hospitals.
  • A mobile application. India is mobile-first and the patient half of this product lives on a phone. A progressive web app at minimum; native for the retention and the notification channel that drives adherence.
  • The dish library from 40 to 500+. Forty dishes is a demo. Five hundred with regional variants and lab-verified nutrition is the moat, and it is the asset a competitor cannot buy.
  • Six more languages. Hindi and English reach roughly 45% of the country. Tamil, Telugu, Bengali, Marathi, Gujarati and Kannada reach most of the rest — and they are exactly the states with the deepest specialist lists.
  • The registered dietitian network as an operation. Poshan Home promises a monthly dietitian review. At 85,000 subscribers that is a staffed operation with rostering, quality control and escalation paths — not a feature. It must be designed as a marketplace or it becomes a margin sink and takes the gross margin down with it.
  • Observability and support tooling. Error tracking, uptime monitoring, an SLA-backed support desk. Enterprise buyers ask for the runbook.
Tier 3 · The ₹1,000 crore unlocks · 12–24 months · needs the Series A

These three change the shape of the business rather than its size. Clinic-by-clinic selling is a good ₹300 crore company. It is not a ₹1,000 crore one, and the next section shows why.

UNLOCK 01

Insurance and corporate wellness

Star Health, Niva Bupa, HDFC Ergo and every large employer run wellness programmes with a nutrition line item and no credible Indian product to fill it. One contract is 80,000 covered lives at roughly ₹350 per life per year. That is B2B2C at a scale no field sales team can reach one clinic at a time.

UNLOCK 02

Pharma patient-support programmes

USV, Abbott, Sun and Lupin already fund diabetes and cardiac adherence programmes. Poshan is a natural fit, the revenue is high-margin and non-dilutive, and each programme arrives with a medical-representative field force that is already inside the clinics we are trying to reach.

UNLOCK 03

A published outcomes study

HbA1c delta at twelve weeks against standard dietary counselling, peer-reviewed and Indian. This is the single asset that turns a hospital procurement committee’s “no” into a “yes,” makes the insurance conversation possible at all, and justifies a premium revenue multiple. Enrol it in month 6.

And the hires that are not on the org chart yet

  • A clinical co-founder or medical director. An Indian health company without a named, credentialled clinician at the top has a ceiling — on hospital sales, on regulator conversations, and on this round’s valuation. This is the most important open role.
  • A VP of Sales who has carried a bag into Indian hospitals. Hired before the reps, not after. Everything in Tier 3 depends on this person existing.
  • A growth lead who owns CAC. The agency executes; someone internal has to own the number, or the ₹3 crore leaves without a cohort report to show for it.
